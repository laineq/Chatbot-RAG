# Diabetes

## Overview

Diabetes is a chronic disease in which the body cannot properly regulate blood glucose (blood sugar) levels. This occurs when the body does not produce enough insulin or cannot effectively use the insulin it produces.

Insulin is a hormone that helps glucose enter cells to be used for energy. When insulin function is impaired, glucose builds up in the bloodstream.

---

## Types of Diabetes

### Type 1 Diabetes
- The body does not produce insulin  
- Caused by autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing cells  
- Usually diagnosed in children or young adults  

### Type 2 Diabetes
- The body does not use insulin properly (insulin resistance)  
- Most common form of diabetes  
- Often associated with lifestyle factors such as obesity and inactivity  

### Gestational Diabetes
- Occurs during pregnancy  
- Usually resolves after childbirth  
- Increases risk of developing Type 2 diabetes later  

---

## Causes and Risk Factors

- Family history of diabetes  
- Overweight or obesity  
- Physical inactivity  
- Unhealthy diet  
- Age (risk increases with age)  
- High blood pressure or cholesterol  

---

## Symptoms

- Increased thirst  
- Frequent urination  
- Extreme hunger  
- Fatigue  
- Blurred vision  
- Slow-healing wounds  

---

## Complications

If not properly managed, diabetes can lead to:

- Heart disease  
- Kidney disease  
- Nerve damage (neuropathy)  
- Eye damage (retinopathy)  
- Foot problems and infections  

---

## Diagnosis

Diabetes is diagnosed using blood tests, including:

- Fasting blood glucose test  
- Oral glucose tolerance test  
- Hemoglobin A1c test  

---

## Treatment

Treatment depends on the type of diabetes but may include:

- Insulin therapy  
- Oral medications  
- Blood sugar monitoring  
- Lifestyle changes (diet and exercise)  

---

## Prevention and Management

- Maintain a healthy weight  
- Eat a balanced diet  
- Exercise regularly  
- Monitor blood sugar levels  
- Follow medical advice and treatment plans
