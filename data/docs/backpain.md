# Back Pain

## Overview

Back pain is a common condition that affects the spine, muscles, and surrounding tissues. It can range from a mild, dull ache to severe or sharp pain and may occur in the upper, middle, or lower back.

Most back pain improves over time with simple treatments, although some cases may become chronic or require medical attention. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

## Causes

Back pain can be caused by a variety of factors, including:

- Muscle or ligament strain (e.g., heavy lifting or sudden movement)  
- Poor posture or prolonged sitting  
- Structural problems in the spine  
- Herniated or ruptured discs  
- Arthritis or degenerative changes  
- Injuries from accidents or sports  

Mechanical or structural issues in the spine (bones, discs, muscles, ligaments, or nerves) are among the most common causes. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of back pain include:

- Dull, aching pain in the back  
- Sharp or stabbing pain  
- Pain that radiates to the legs or arms  
- Stiffness or reduced mobility  
- Muscle spasms  

Severity can range from mild discomfort to disabling pain.

---

## Types of Back Pain

Back pain is often categorized by duration:

- **Acute:** lasts a few days to weeks  
- **Subacute:** lasts several weeks  
- **Chronic:** lasts longer than 3 months  

---

## Diagnosis

Healthcare providers evaluate back pain by:

- Asking about symptoms, frequency, and severity  
- Performing a physical examination  
- Determining possible causes based on history and risk factors  

In some cases, imaging tests such as MRI may be used to assess structural issues in the spine. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

## Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and severity of the pain.

### Common treatments include:

- Rest (short-term only)  
- Heat or cold therapy  
- Over-the-counter pain relievers (e.g., ibuprofen, acetaminophen)  
- Physical therapy and exercise  
- Lifestyle adjustments  

Most cases improve with conservative treatments. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

### Additional treatments (if needed):

- Prescription medications  
- Injections  
- Complementary therapies (e.g., acupuncture, chiropractic care)  
- Surgery (in severe cases) :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}  

---

## Self-Care and Management

You can manage or reduce back pain by:

- Staying active (avoid prolonged bed rest)  
- Applying ice first, then heat  
- Maintaining good posture  
- Exercising regularly  
- Avoiding heavy lifting or improper movement  

Resting too long can actually worsen back pain. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}  

---

## When to See a Doctor

Seek medical attention if:

- Pain is severe or does not improve after a few days  
- Pain occurs after an injury  
- There is numbness, weakness, or loss of function  
- Pain interferes with daily activities  

Healthcare providers will assess the cause and recommend appropriate treatment. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}  

---

## Prevention

- Maintain a healthy weight  
- Exercise regularly  
- Use proper lifting techniques  
- Improve posture  
- Use ergonomic work setups
